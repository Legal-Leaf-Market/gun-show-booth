# Market research brief for Gemini: Booth Bandits

## 0. How to use this brief

You are doing desk research for **Booth Bandits**, a two-person vendor table
in central Indiana (based around Indianapolis and Columbus, IN). They sell
**handmade coasters, home-baked goods and brand-new merch** (patches, hats,
tees, stickers) at **gun shows** and **pop-up markets** (Fountain Square
markets, flea markets, holiday markets). They are **not a firearms dealer**
and never buy, sell or transfer guns, ammo or gun parts. They have a simple
website (goods gallery, events calendar with map, "text us to hold it") and
want to know what to sell, where to set up, what it costs, what rules apply,
and how comparable sellers present themselves online.

Work through the sections in order. Section 1 holds the rules for your
answers, and they matter more than coverage.

---

## 1. Rules for the research

1. **Every fact gets a source.** A URL plus the date you read it. Put
   sources inline in each table row, not just in a list at the end.
2. **Label every claim** as one of:
   - **Verified**: read on an official or primary source (state statute,
     agency page, the show organizer's own site, a company's own fee page).
   - **Reported**: from a secondary source (news, forum, blog, review site).
   - **Inferred**: your reasoning from the above. Say what it's based on.
   Never present inferred or reported as verified. This business publishes
   what you find on a public website and on printed labels, and a wrong
   "fact" there is worse than a gap.
3. **Say what you could not find.** "No fee published; call required" is a
   useful answer. A guessed number is not.
4. **Dates and prices go stale.** Give the year for every price, fee, show
   date and rule. Flag anything older than 18 months as possibly stale.
5. **Indiana first.** Answer for Indiana and the Indianapolis metro before
   generalising. Note where a county or city rule (Marion, Bartholomew,
   Johnson, Hamilton counties; Indianapolis, Columbus) differs from the state.
6. **No legal advice framing.** Report what the rules say and who enforces
   them, then list the questions the owners should confirm with the agency or
   an attorney. Don't tell them they are "fine".
7. **Write plainly.** No em dashes (house style for everything this business
   publishes). Short sentences. Tables wherever a comparison is involved.
8. **Don't invent competitors or quotes.** If you summarise a business's
   reviews, link the page and give the count you saw.

---

## 2. Deliverable format

One Markdown report, sections numbered to match section 3 below, each
starting with a 3–5 bullet **"What this means for Booth Bandits"** summary,
then the detail. Finish with:

- **Section A: Top 10 actions**, ranked by money or risk, each pointing to
  the finding behind it.
- **Section B: Open questions to confirm by phone or email**, with who to ask
  (agency, show organizer, venue) and their published contact.
- **Section C: Source list** with access dates.

Include CSV-ready tables (pipe tables are fine) for the show calendar,
vendor fees and payment processors, so they can be pasted into a spreadsheet
or into the site's `data/events.json`.

---

## 3. Research questions

### 3.1 The Indiana gun show circuit (highest priority)

1. List every **recurring gun show and gun-and-knife show** within about 2
   hours of Indianapolis (Indiana, plus Louisville KY, Cincinnati and Dayton
   OH, and eastern Illinois). For each:

   | Show name | Organizer | Venue | Address | City | Typical schedule (e.g. "2nd weekend monthly") | Next 6 known dates | Hours | Public admission price | Who gets in free | Table fee (per 8 ft / 6 ft) | Non-firearm vendors allowed? | Food sales allowed? | Booking contact | Source |

2. Who are the **main organizers** in the region (promoters who run several
   shows)? How do they take vendor bookings (online form, phone, Facebook)?
   How far ahead do tables sell out? Are there waitlists or "regular vendor"
   priority spots?
3. **Rules for non-firearm vendors**: does each organizer allow crafts,
   baked goods and apparel? Any category caps (e.g. one jerky vendor per
   show)? Rules on **food**: many venues have exclusive concession contracts
   that ban outside food sales, including packaged baked goods. Find these
   rules per venue (Indiana State Fairgrounds, county fairgrounds, expo
   centres, VFW and American Legion halls).
4. **Vendor requirements per show**: proof of insurance (and minimum
   coverage), Indiana Retail Merchant Certificate, setup/teardown times,
   electricity fees, table/chair rental, tablecloth rules, parking, early
   entry.
5. **Attendance**: published or reported attendance figures and how they
   vary by season. Which months are strongest? (Holiday season and tax-refund
   season are commonly cited; verify.)
6. Admission norms: what's the typical price range in Indiana in 2025–2026?
   Which groups commonly get in free or discounted (seniors, veterans,
   police, first responders, kids, women's days)? This feeds the site's
   default admission text, which currently says "$6, seniors, police and
   first responders free". Is that typical?

### 3.3 What sells at these events

1. For **non-firearm vendors at gun shows**: which product categories show up
   most and appear to do well (jerky, knives, leather goods, holsters
   excluded because they're gun accessories, apparel, patches, flags,
   candles, honey, kettle corn, signs, coasters, woodworking, soaps)? Price
   points that move. Evidence from vendor interviews, forums
   (r/Flipping, r/craftsale, r/EtsySellers, vendor Facebook groups), news
   features. Label each Reported or Inferred.
2. For **coasters** specifically (wood, slate, resin/epoxy, leather,
   engraved): typical retail prices at markets and on Etsy for a set of 4,
   personalised vs not, best sellers (state outlines, pets, sports teams,
   hunting/fishing themes, cabins). What sells to a gun show crowd versus a
   Fountain Square crowd?
3. For **baked goods** at markets: price ranges per item, bundle pricing,
   shelf life norms, packaging expectations, what sells in cold months.
4. For **merch** (patches, hats, tees, stickers): what designs and price
   points do small brands sell at these events? Is a "brand merch" line
   viable for an unknown brand, or does it need a theme (Indiana, outdoors,
   humour)?
5. **Average transaction** and **sales per show** reported by comparable
   small vendors, so the owners can judge whether a $X table fee pays off.
   Build a simple break-even table: table fee + gas + costs vs items needed
   to sell at each price point.

### 3.4 Rules that apply to this business (Indiana)

Report the rule, the citation, the agency, and what to confirm.

1. **Home-baked goods**: Indiana's home-based vendor law (look up IC
   16-42-5-29 and any amendments, including the 2022 changes). What foods
   qualify (non-potentially-hazardous), where they may be sold (farmers
   markets, roadside stands, events, online?), **required label wording**
   (exact statement), food handler certification requirement, any annual
   sales cap, whether shipping is allowed, county health department role in
   Marion and Bartholomew counties. The site and the printed labels will use
   whatever you find, so quote the statute's wording exactly and link it.
2. **Sales tax**: Indiana Retail Merchant Certificate (cost, how to apply on
   INTIME), collecting 7% sales tax at shows, filing frequency, whether event
   organizers collect or report vendor info to the Department of Revenue,
   whether baked goods are taxable (food for home consumption vs prepared
   food rules), whether handmade items are taxable.
3. **Business setup**: DBA / assumed business name filing in Indiana (county
   recorder vs Secretary of State), LLC cost, whether a sole proprietor can
   operate under "Booth Bandits" without filing, EIN.
4. **Insurance**: vendor liability insurance options for craft and market
   vendors (products liability for food), typical annual vs per-event cost,
   providers commonly accepted by Indiana shows (find names and published
   prices; do not recommend one).
5. **Gun show specifics for a non-dealer vendor**: anything in Indiana or
   federal rules that affects someone who is *not* selling firearms at a gun
   show (there probably isn't, but check whether any shows require all
   vendors to sign firearm-related agreements). Also: are any merch or
   gift items commonly sold at these shows **regulated in Indiana** (knives,
   brass knuckles, pepper spray, tasers, "solvent trap" kits, laser
   devices)? The owners want to know what to avoid listing.
6. **Online "hold for pickup"**: any rule issue with taking deposits or
   holding baked goods ordered by text for pickup at a show?

### 3.5 Payments

1. Compare card processing for in-person market vendors as of 2026:

   | Processor | Hardware cost | In-person rate | Keyed rate | Offline mode? | Payout speed | Monthly fee | Restricted-business notes relevant to gun-show attendance | Source |

   Include Square, Stripe Terminal / Tap to Pay, SumUp, Clover Go, PayPal
   Zettle (if still offered in the US), Shopify POS.
2. **Restricted-business policies**: do any of these processors restrict

5. Cell coverage problem: many expo halls have poor signal. Which readers
   work offline and what are the limits?

### 3.6 How comparable sellers show up online

1. Find **15–25 small vendors** who sell at gun shows or Midwest markets
   *without* selling firearms (coasters, woodworking, jerky, apparel, candles,
   baked goods). For each: website URL, platform (Shopify, Square Online,
   Etsy, Linktree only, Facebook only), whether they list upcoming shows,
   how (calendar, list, Facebook events), whether they show prices, whether
   they offer hold-for-pickup or preorders, social channels and approximate
   follower counts (as displayed, with date).
2. What features do the **best** of them have that Booth Bandits' site does
   not? The current site has: goods with photos and prices, a "text us about
   this" button, an events calendar with a map, one page per show with
   directions and add-to-calendar, admission info, a printable table sign
   with QR code, and a phone admin. Candidates to evaluate: email list,
   preorder deposits, wishlist, "notify me when restocked", reviews,
   loyalty punch card, gift cards, custom order form, local delivery.
   Recommend at most five, ranked by effort vs payoff for a two-person
   table.
3. Look at **gun show organizer websites and listing directories** (for
   example sites that aggregate gun show dates nationally). How do they list
   shows, what data fields do they use, do they accept vendor listings or
   "where to find us" links, and is a listing free? The site already
   publishes show dates with schema.org Event markup; would these
   directories or Google pick that up?
4. **Name and brand availability** for the shortlisted names: Booth Bandits,
   Hot Creek Trading Co., Mule Train Mercantile, Rumrunner Supply Co., Dead
   Drop Supply, Smokewagon Goods, Side Hustle Syndicate, Bootlegger's Table,
   Contraband Co., The Plug Trading Co. For each: USPTO trademark search
   (live marks in related classes: 21 coasters, 25 apparel, 30 baked goods,
   35 retail), Indiana business entity search, `.com` and `.shop`
   availability (as reported by a registrar search, dated), Instagram /
   Facebook / TikTok handle availability. Flag any name already used by a
   business in Indiana or by a firearms-related business (which would cause
   the exact confusion they want to avoid).
